# Phase 9.2: Research Claims Audit & Evidentiary Boundaries

## 1. Governance Principles & Evidentiary Standard

This claims matrix provides an auditable, scientifically conservative evaluation of all potential research claims regarding the IFSI architecture and the **Phase 7 Config B Research Candidate**.

In accordance with strict empirical reporting guidelines:
- Every claim is assigned an explicit **Evidence Strength** tier based solely on existing artifacts.
- "Strong" is strictly scoped to the evaluated benchmark suites ($N \le 500$) and **never** extrapolated to claim unproven real-world operational efficacy.
- Claims lacking empirical verification on real-world email streams are explicitly designated **`Unsupported`** or **`Not evaluated`**.
- Precise permitted and prohibited wording guides future publications and technical reports.

## 3. Scientific Reporting Guidelines for Publications

When drafting scholarly publications, technical reports, or external documentation based on IFSI findings:

1. **State the Exact Denominator**: Always cite the exact sample volume ($N$) and class distribution (e.g. *"99.58% accuracy on a 240-email challenge suite comprising 130 legitimate and 110 phishing samples"*).
2. **Explicitly Label Inferences**:
   - **Observed Result**: An empirical data point directly recorded in a verified prediction artifact (e.g. *"Config B remediated 3 of 3 historical false positives"*).
   - **Interpretation**: A logical deduction derived from an observed result (e.g. *"This indicates that public suffix root extraction prevents false lookalike warnings on secondary domains"*).
   - **Hypothesis**: An unverified proposition requiring future testing (e.g. *"We hypothesize that this mechanism will reduce false positives on uncurated corporate mailboxes"*).
3. **Disclose Benchmark Enriched Distributions**: Acknowledge that challenge suites have artificially high attack densities ($\sim 50\%$) designed to probe failure boundaries, contrasting with real-world enterprise environments where phishing represents $< 1\%$ of inbound volume.
